Security in Loudoun County

Loudoun County combines fast-growing suburbs, historic towns, farms, vineyards, a large concentration of data centers, and a major international airport, Washington Dulles International Airport. It also has a significant presence of federal government offices, federal government contractors, technology companies, and aerospace companies. Loudoun serves as both a bedroom community of Washington, DC, and an employment hub on its own.

That mix creates a wide range of security needs, including:

  • Restaurants, breweries, and wineries: late-night crowds, large events, and cash handling.
  • Schools and childcare facilities: emergency plans for threats, medical emergencies, and missing children.
  • Nonprofits and houses of worship: open-door gatherings, large events, and donations.
  • Offices, medical and law practices, and contractors: sensitive information, valuable equipment, and insider threats.
  • Retail stores and shopping centers: shoplifting, organized retail crime, and payment fraud.
  • Families and individuals: threats, harassment, home security, and fraud, forgery, impersonation, and other scams.

Crime Trends in Loudoun County

Crime in Loudoun County is generally low and fell overall in 2025, but fraud-related crimes and scams are increasing. Fraud-type offenses, such as false pretenses, credit card fraud, identity theft, forgery, and impersonation, make up 18.7% of all offenses in Loudoun County, more than seven percentage points higher than Virginia as a whole, and they were up 6% from 2024 to 2025. Across the Sheriff's Office and the three town police departments, forgery and counterfeiting offenses are up 58%, from 135 to 213, and impersonation offenses are up 27%, from 156 to 198, while wire fraud offenses are up 13%. Source for these crime statistics

The practical risk for most residents and businesses is financial fraud and identity crime, such as forged checks, impersonation scams, and account takeovers, not violent crime.

As a Certified Fraud Examiner, I help organizations and families spot these schemes, tighten the controls that stop them, and investigate when one succeeds. Crime in the Town Police Jurisdictions

  • Leesburg: White collar crime increased from 2024 to 2025, including a 36% increase in forgery offenses and a 25% increase in impersonation offenses.
  • Middleburg and Purcellville: The relatively low number of offenses in each town makes percentage-based analysis potentially unreliable. Even so, white collar crimes such as fraud, credit card fraud, wire fraud, and forgery generally increased from 2024 to 2025, while violent crime decreased.
