Common Situations

  • An employee, customer, or former partner has made a threat, or their behavior worries you.
  • You are ending a relationship with an employee, a client, or a business partner, and you are concerned about how that person will respond.
  • Someone keeps contacting you after you asked them to stop, or you believe you are being stalked or followed.
  • Someone is harassing or intimidating you, your family, or your organization online.
  • A social media post or message comes from someone who worries you, or it contains content you perceive as a threat, intimidation, or coercion.
  • A dispute with a neighbor, vendor, or member of your community is escalating.
  • You are not sure whether what is happening is a threat, and you want a professional opinion.

What Is a Threat Assessment?

A threat assessment is a structured look at a threatening or concerning situation to decide how serious it is and what to do about it. Most threats are never carried out. However, only an assessment and investigation can determine the true intentions behind a threat and the likelihood that it will be carried out. I gather the facts, assess the level of risk, develop a comprehensive plan to reduce the risk, and then work with you to decide what steps you want to take.

Threat assessments can also be conducted for workplace violence prevention, stalking, harassment, and other concerning behavior.

Anyone facing a threat or concerning behavior can request one, including businesses, nonprofits, schools, houses of worship, families, and individuals.

You do not have to decide alone whether something is serious. I will listen, tell you what I see, and explain your options.

What You Receive

A Written Report

My report will address the risks, warning signs of escalation, and whether the subject is capable of carrying out the threats.

A Threat Mitigation Plan

A plan that identifies definitive actions that can be taken to reduce the risk. If appropriate, the plan identifies tripwires that indicate an updated threat assessment is needed and recommends whether a regular review on a monthly, quarterly, or annual basis is appropriate.

I investigate what happened by conducting interviews, collecting evidence, and developing facts. I use this information to prepare my written report and a threat mitigation plan. We review them together, and I help you determine how to respond and carry out the plan, including emergency procedures where needed, with the goal of reducing the threat level over time.

5

Reevaluate

I reevaluate the threat on a scheduled basis or whenever the facts and circumstances of your situation require it. I review new communications and interactions and conduct a full review of open-source intelligence and proprietary databases to determine whether new information materially affects the threat assessment and requires an update. Reevaluation can also be part of a Security Director on Demand contract.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I know if it is a threat?

If you feel that you, your family, your business, or your organization is being intimidated, coerced, or harassed, you may be experiencing a threat. If you or someone else is in immediate danger, call 9-1-1 immediately. Once the immediate danger is over, contact me to discuss whether a threat assessment is appropriate for your circumstances.

What if my situation is urgent?

If you or someone else is in immediate danger, call 9-1-1 first. For an urgent threat that does not involve an immediate threat to life or safety, call or text me at 571-643-2338, or message me on WhatsApp or Signal.

Will you contact the person making the threat?

Whether I contact the subject depends on several factors, including the involvement of law enforcement, the nature of the threat, and your wishes. We discuss any contact with the subject regularly during our meetings about the threat.

Will the police be involved?

If a law has been broken, local law enforcement may be involved. The level of involvement depends on the nature of the threat, the available evidence, and the availability of police resources. As a licensed private investigator in Virginia, I am authorized to conduct criminal and civil investigations, conduct surveillance, and gather evidence in support of potential criminal or civil judicial action. I am also authorized to conduct background investigations. At your request, I can conduct investigations independently of law enforcement action.

Can you work with my HR team or attorney?

Yes. In threat situations involving an employee or contractor, I almost always work with HR teams, ownership, attorneys, and others. When planning threat assessments and investigations, I often work directly with HR to determine what evidence is needed to take administrative action against the subject.

Do you provide bodyguards or protective services?

No. Personal protection in Virginia requires a separate qualification and license from the Department of Criminal Justice Services. If you would like, I can help you find and work with a properly licensed personal protection firm.

How much does it cost?

Costs depend on the nature of the threat and the services requested. For example, a threat or harassment that occurs online generally costs less than an in-person threat by a known subject.

I begin with a flat upfront fee that covers a meeting or interview with you and any victim, collection of initial evidence, meetings with any witnesses, initial steps to identify the subject and conduct an initial background assessment, and coordination with law enforcement as appropriate.

I then meet with you to discuss the need for, and the cost of, a threat assessment, a complete threat investigation, a threat mitigation plan, support to implement the plan, and threat reevaluation. By mutual consent, we then enter into a fixed-cost contract for the services you need.
