Security Needs in the City of Falls Church

The City of Falls Church is compact and walkable, with a busy commercial corridor, a year-round farmers market, its own public schools, and many small businesses. That creates a wide range of needs, including:

  • Small businesses, restaurants, and shops: theft, payment fraud, cash handling, and employee conduct.
  • Professional, legal, and financial offices: confidential client information, fraud, and insider misconduct.
  • Schools, childcare facilities, and camps: emergency plans for threats, medical emergencies, and missing children.
  • Houses of worship and nonprofits: open-door gatherings, community events, volunteers, and donations.
  • Event organizers and venues: emergency planning and visitor safety.
  • Families and individuals: threats, harassment, home security, and scams that target residents.

Crime and Fraud Risks in the City of Falls Church

The City of Falls Church has its own police department, so its crime is reported separately from Fairfax County figures. Fraud, theft, and scams affect every community in Northern Virginia, and one incident can cause a large loss for a small business or a family. For county-wide figures, see my Fairfax County crime trends.

Fraud crimes reported to the Falls Church Police Department held steady in 2025, at 68 in both years. Larceny reports rose about 2%, to 208 from 204, and shoplifting rose 21%, from 76 to 92, which is about 44% of all larceny. Theft from buildings was the next most common type, at 60 reports. Burglary fell from 12 reports to six, and motor vehicle theft stayed about the same, at 17 reports compared with 16.

Violent crime stayed low, with 15 reports in 2024 and 11 in 2025. The 2025 reports included five aggravated assaults, five robberies, and one rape.

Fraud and theft can reach any business or family in the City of Falls Church.
