Missing Persons

I work missing person investigations for families, attorneys, businesses, and organizations. I work alongside law enforcement and coordinate on the investigation when appropriate. If you have not already, I encourage you to report the person missing to law enforcement. If you believe a missing person is in immediate danger, call 9-1-1 first.

Skip Tracing

Skip tracing is the work of finding someone who cannot be reached. I use investigative methods to locate a person, including public records, proprietary databases, and interviews. I handle every matter in confidence.

Cases I Decline

I do not accept a skip tracing or missing person investigation if I determine that locating the person and sharing that information could put that person at risk. Such cases include matters involving allegations of domestic violence, sexual abuse, or child abuse between the client and the person.

What I Need to Begin

To start a skip tracing or missing person investigation, I need:

  • An understanding of your connection to the person and the purpose of the search.
  • A signed contract for the service.
  • The person's name, age, birthday, place of birth, last known address, historical addresses if known, email addresses, phone numbers, and any other contact information, even if it no longer works. Recent photos of the person are extremely helpful.

What You Receive

I report the location and contact information to you or your representative, along with a written summary of the investigation. I provide case updates on a regular basis, and I am available to discuss the case with you.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between skip tracing and a missing person investigation?

Skip tracing locates someone who cannot be reached, often to recover money or property. A missing person investigation helps a family or an attorney find someone who is missing. Both use similar investigative methods.

Can you promise to find the person?

No. I cannot promise a result; however, I will use all lawful techniques to attempt to find the person and confirm the person's identity before providing you with a result. The contract will spell out the efforts and deliverables.

How long does a search take?

The length of a search is difficult to predict, so I do not provide a timeline for closing a case.

Do you locate anyone who asks?

No. Before I begin, I need to understand your connection to the person and the purpose of the search. I do not accept a skip tracing or missing person investigation if I determine that locating the person and sharing that information could put that person at risk.

Do you work with law enforcement?

Yes. I work alongside law enforcement and coordinate on the investigation when appropriate.

How much does it cost?

The cost depends on the scope of the search. A written contract sets out my efforts, the deliverables, and the terms, and payment is due before I render services.
