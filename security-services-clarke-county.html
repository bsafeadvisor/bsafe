Security in Clarke County

Clarke County is Virginia's smallest county by area, with a historic county seat, working farms, vineyards, and a steady stream of visitors on the Appalachian Trail and the Shenandoah River. It sits on the edge of the Washington commuter area, so many residents work elsewhere and leave properties empty during the day.

Those conditions make planning matter. A review of access to your property, a clear emergency plan, and a trusted advisor you can call quickly help owners of farms, estates, shops, and homes decide what to do before something happens.

Clarke County at a Glance

  • Clarke County was formed in 1836 from part of Frederick County, and Berryville is the county seat. Source for this fact about Clarke County
  • The county covers about 178 square miles and had 14,783 residents at the 2020 census. Source for this fact about Clarke County
  • Clarke County borders Loudoun, Fauquier, Warren, and Frederick Counties in Virginia and Jefferson County, West Virginia. Source for this fact about Clarke County
  • Berryville sits at the junction of U.S. Route 340 and Virginia Route 7, about 60 miles west of Washington, D.C. Source for this fact about Clarke County
  • Berryville was chartered on January 15, 1798, and its historic downtown is a focus of the Berryville Main Street organization. Source for this fact about Clarke County
