Security Needs in Fairfax County

Fairfax County organizations often handle sensitive information, employ many people, and welcome the public every day. That creates a wide range of needs, including:

  • Professional, legal, and financial offices: client records, money handling, fraud, and insider misconduct.
  • Medical and dental offices: patient privacy, staff safety, and workplace threats.

  • Families and individuals: threats, harassment, home security, and fraud and other scams.

Crime Trends in Fairfax County

Violent crime reported to the Fairfax County Police Department rose about 12% in 2025, to 1,248 victims from about 1,115 in 2024. Aggravated assault is driving the increase: aggravated assault victims are up 35%, from about 541 to 731, and about four in five victims with a known relationship knew the offender. Robbery fell 11%, from about 404 to 359, and murder and rape also fell. Police cleared about 49% of aggravated assaults in 2025, down from 59% in 2024.

Fraud is the larger trend in the offense reports. Fraud crimes reported to the department rose about 15% in 2025, to 3,548 from 3,088. False pretenses, swindle, and confidence game offenses rose 21%, from 1,168 to 1,413, and credit card and ATM fraud rose 9%, from 455 to 495. Impersonation reports fell 19%, from 1,385 to 1,121, but the department also began recording identity theft separately in 2025, so I read the individual fraud categories with caution. About 92% of fraud victims were individuals, and about two-thirds of the fraud happened at a home, so families are the most common targets. Only about 3.5% of fraud crimes were cleared, which means most victims need to protect themselves and investigate on their own.

Other property crime fell. Burglary dropped 25%, from 500 to 375, and motor vehicle theft dropped 7%, from 1,255 to 1,161. Robbery reports fell from 404 to 360. The share of crimes cleared also fell in every category, including burglary, from 37% to 30%, and larceny, from 33% to 25%.

Reported theft was about flat. Larceny reports fell 1%, from 15,051 to 14,876, and shoplifting was 8,200 of them, more than half. About two-thirds of larceny happened at commercial locations. Police made 37% more shoplifting arrests in 2025, from 293 to 402, even though total arrests fell 13%, from 19,794 to 17,178. Fraud arrests fell 14%, from 1,003 to 866, while reported fraud rose.
