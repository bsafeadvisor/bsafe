Security and Investigation Needs in Fairfax County

Fairfax County organizations often handle sensitive information, employ many people, and welcome the public every day. That creates a wide range of needs, including:

  • Professional, legal, and financial offices: client records, money handling, fraud, and insider misconduct.
  • Medical and dental offices: patient privacy, staff safety, and workplace threats.
  • Retail stores, restaurants, and bars: shoplifting, robbery, cash handling, closing routines, and payment fraud.
  • Government contractors and technology companies: sensitive information, valuable equipment, background investigations, and insider threats.
  • Schools, childcare facilities, and houses of worship: emergency plans for threats, medical emergencies, and missing children.
  • Families and individuals: threats, harassment, home security, and fraud and other scams.

Crime Trends in Fairfax County

Violent crime reported to the Fairfax County Police Department rose about 12% from 2024 to 2025, including a 35% increase in aggravated assaults. Source: Fairfax County Police Department crime dashboard, 2025 compared with 2024 for violent crime

Fraud related crimes reported to the department rose about 15% from 2024 to 2025.

About 92% of fraud victims are individuals, and about two-thirds of fraud happens at a home, so families are the most common targets. Fairfax County Police reported that only 3.5% of fraud related crimes were cleared, which means victims need to protect themselves and may benefit from private investigation services to gather evidence to recover lost funds. Source: Virginia State Police NIBRS, calendar year 2025 compared with 2024 for these crime statistics

Based on the data, some of the largest risks faced by Fairfax County families, businesses, and organizations are fraud, theft, and threats.
